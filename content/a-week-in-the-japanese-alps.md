Summary: Bikepacking through the Japanese alps is maybe the only bikepacking route you need.

Steven and I discuss they day's itinerary over oat milk lattes at Paddler's Coffee in Tokyo. We were already in a good mood because they remembered us. Regular status. Paddler's is a great place to have a quiet morning and maybe pet a dog.

That day we needed to get to Shinjuku station, pack our bikes into rinko bags, board a train destined for Nishi-Matsuida where we start. Then we rebuild our bikes and start climbing. 

We find a relatively quiet corner outside of Shinjuku, take apart our bikes, put them in rinko bags, and get going.

Steven's routes are some of the best I've ever ridden. The first day we saw some incredible mountain roads and incredible brickwork on railroad infrastructure spiraling up the mountain. We stayed in Karuizawa the first night, which was way too fancy for either of us. We are, however, both huge Terrace House fans so we had to stay near where they filmed the season that takes place in Nagano.

Our accomodations were not fancy: we were in a backpacking hostel that was very cool and very chill. The manager Yusuke confirmed the sickness of Steven's routes and gave us some places to check out along the way. 

![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNGh_8WF2JvjSDrg38EY8Kc1Guf1FZ7VSFZ18UCZhIbezU1djzRsIQUMHqvaVgAOKLhHNiCmZrEHsGGTvKT-iUdrGTBvlynPrtQwfzlqPGtOcHcrCFdf24wL31rQUe4ylSJbioFzCwDw5EKdYs4I5tojw=w1998-h1498-s-no?authuser=0)

We took some trains to avoid the cold rain the night before, and ended up approaching Lake Shirakaba via the Venus Line: a mountain route with amazing mountain views on the way up. We stopped in the world's fanciest konbini and continued to Utsukushigahara, an area of highlands where we saw a bunch of cows and talked to some high schoolers about our bike trip (Steven talked to them in Japanese and I tried to look friendly). The descent out of Utsukushigahara included a short gravel section and then 12 uninterrupted miles of descending on fresh pavement with very few cars. It was some of the most fun I've ever had on a bike.

We stayed in another backpacking hostel in Matsumoto and started our day by dropping off unneeded weight at the Yamoto to send along to our hotel in a couple of days. The person who helped us was so sweet and kind and helped us address the box properly with nicely written kanji. Steven told her all about our travels in Japanese. 

![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczO1p9P8uT--2hetpBe46ydzTyI_lv7zONXWZc_TTqf7PftgvPVveYEf-iyOsambh5TGtODBSPNDO97rNwytAIFyG2SiBOan-OhNZjn2iSWsswt6oJQt0GOI9bLtyzUaXzMoRuRnx6FhzgjHucOJvxAzYA=w1124-h1498-s-no?authuser=0)

We had a long climb up route 158 with a stream next to us that was both vibrantly teal and clear. Our descent into Hirayu was cold and wet but very fast and fun. We got into our accommodations dripping from the mist outside. Our hotelier rushed to get us out of our dripping wet jackets and shoes and get us checked into our room. We had a private onsen connected to our room that night that warmed us up from the cold ride down the mountain.

The climb up Mt. Norikura had three distinct phases: waterfalls at the base, the woods from Twilight in the middle, and grass and an alpine lake above the tree line. This was the third consecutive day of breaking my lifetime climbing record and I was dragging. At some points I was going three miles an hour. Once we got over Norikura, we descended for 14 miles, transitioning from fall colors down to sunny green mountain towns. 

The next two days we wended our way through the Kiso valley. We met a couple from Bristol bikepacking along the same route and ran into a New Zealander an improbable 4 times in two days. Both were so excited to share where they came from and where they were going and asked the same from us. 

![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczOLnnaETzch13yXHDXzWq0nnms0vSHyRUIZKa3NcoLrhp-sB0SPKHUL1pIbjpCRZPIiPc-rQDh0cVLI9LAqqELn5uOfh7qP3stBb9YyMoGLr00hUzheXaIhhzqZ-VDG9L1O9SRvfLdwyAyaLP2NX256zg=w1124-h1498-s-no?authuser=0)

We ended our time in the Kiso valley biking the Sapphire Road into the Atera valley. The entire ride we were along a crystal mountain river speckled with huge boulders. We ended at a campsite right on the river that rushed all night and lulled us to sleep. 



The next morning we descended the Sapphire road and incredibly ran into our New Zealand friend again at the bottom. We were leaving the region that day, so we had to say our goodbyes but I still think about the odds against running into someone so often across so much land. 

We left Nagano via the Shinkansen, traveling at around 170 mph towards Kyoto.
