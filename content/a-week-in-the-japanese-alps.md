Summary: Bikepacking through the Japanese alps is maybe the only bikepacking route you need.

Steven and I discuss they day's itinerary over an oat milk latte at Paddler's Coffee in Tokyo. We were already in a good mood because they remembered us. Regular status.

That day we needed to get to Shinjuku station, pack our bikes into rinko bags, board a train destined for Nishi-Matsuida where we start. Then we rebuild our bikes and start climbing.

Steven's routes are incredible. The first day we saw some incredible mountain roads and incredible brickwork on railroad infrastructure spiraling up the mountain. We stayed in Karuizawa the first night, which was way too fancy for either of us. It was, however, the filming location of a season of Terrace House, so we had to check it out. 



Our accomodations were not fancy: we were in a backpacking hostel that was very cool and very chill. 



We took some trains to avoid the cold rain the night before, and ended up approaching Lake Shirakaba via the Venus Line: a mountain route with amazing mountain views on the way up. We stopped in the world's fanciest konbini and continued to Utsukushigahara, an area of highlands where we saw a bunch of cows and talked to some high schoolers about our bike trip (Steven talked to them in Japanese and I tried to look friendly). The descent out of Utsukushigahara included a short gravel section and then 12 uninterrupted miles of descending on fresh pavement with very few cars. It was some of the most fun I've ever had on a bike.

We stayed in another backpacking hostel in Matsumoto and started our day by dropping off unneeded weight at the Yamoto to send along to our hotel in a couple of days. The person who helped us was so sweet and kind and helped us address the box properly. Steven told her all about our travels in Japanese and I tried to look friendly. We had a long climb up route 158 with a stream next to us that was both vibrantly teal and crystal clear. Our descent into Hirayu was cold and wet. We got into our accommodations dripping from the mist outside, and our hotelier was so kind in getting us dry. We had a private onsen connected to our room that night that warmed us up from the cold ride down the mountain. 



The climb up Mt. Norikura had three distinct phases: waterfalls at the base, woods from Twilight in the middle, and an alpine lake above the tree line. This was the third consecutive day of breaking my climbing record and I was dragging. At some points I was going three miles an hour. Once we got over Norikura, we descended for 14 miles through fall colors down to sunny mountain towns. 

The next two days we wended our way through the Kiso valley, ending in an unreal campsite next to a deep blue stream that was rushing all night.
